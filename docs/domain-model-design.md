# Domain model audit and design

Audit base: `61b265ec26ca0e6ff4bc9a49cb43ca3b8153c335`. This review challenges the business model and its actual consumers; existing descriptions are hypotheses to check against behavior. The separate [design QA](domain-model-design-qa.md) precedes implementation.

## Findings and decisions

| Area | Decision and evidence | Downstream effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product scope and business record | Retain the thirteen assertion fields, immutable exact case reference, separate technical identity and independent closure/payment axes. No requirement justifies adding coverage, banking, partial payment, currency conversion or a generic workflow engine. Public views may be untrusted; only opaque Claim represents accepted business state. | Public/native/wire shapes and record encoding remain meaningful and unchanged. |
| Scalar types | Retain exact raw authored strings at the input/identity boundary and parsed DateOnly/decimal values inside accepted state. Amount grammar fits decimal without rounding; currency syntax deliberately does not assert ISO membership. Derive the amount grammar/text budget and currency grammar from their declared digit counts instead of repeating numeric limits. Unicode scalar length, strict UTF-8, ordinal references and historical date bounds remain intentional. | Existing scalar properties, canonical vectors and boundary decoders qualify these decisions. No global normalization or floating-point amount conversion. |
| Parsed command admission | Admission currently parses payloads and discards their typed values; transitions parse them again. Introduce one internal validated command representation per decision invocation. Public validateRequest projects that same parser to unit. | Application still validates before hashing and Domain still validates at authoritative execution: these are separate trust boundaries, not redundant permissions to remove. Preserve malformed-input refusal ordering. |
| Correction representation | Corrections turn accepted state into CaseFields, reconstruct raw groups, track independent Replaced/Cleared booleans, validate repeatedly, then restore Claim. Operate on typed facts/progress and validated tagged correction groups instead. KEEP consumes current accepted values; status/reference/revision stay outside correction outputs. | Remove the raw correction-resolution round trip and boolean choice records. Preserve explicit paid-decision acknowledgement, existing-value requirements, atomicity and logical no-op refusal. |
| Accepted-state invariants | Restoration and correction separately implement decision/payment chronology and positive paid amounts. One internal state validator serves restoration and every successful transition. | No partial decision tuple enters accepted progress; no paid state has zero amount or payment before decision. The final candidate is validated before constructing opaque Claim and returning a new revision. |
| Business dates | Native probes demonstrate that unchanged accepted notification/decision dates are rejected by ordinary amendment/decision replacement after clock rollback, although the published contract and correction path allow reaffirmation. Validate each newly asserted date against today; unchanged accepted dates remain historical facts. | Amend checks incident and notification independently, so a new future incident cannot hide behind an unchanged later notification. Adding a decision or payment always asserts a new date. Chronological order still applies to the complete result. |
| Semantic discovery | Closed-case and registration-amendment descriptions omit the correction exception. Correct their scope and bump DomainRules.version for the actual behavior correction. | Regenerate the semantic/CLI/Web contract lock and update generated-DTO test fixtures. Do not change canonical request bytes merely because executable rule meaning changes. |
| Stored rule evidence | SQL writers/readers, encrypted witness candidates and primary/witness matching independently hard-code rule revision 1. Bind every producer and reader to DomainRules.version and update the fresh baseline constraint to the reviewed revision. | Regenerate checksum and actual PostgreSQL catalog. Unsupported old baselines remain refused untouched; no migration, adoption or compatibility dispatch. |
| Revision range | Domain refuses Int64.MaxValue, while current-case and accepted-history SQL only require positive revisions. Tighten those two structural constraints to the representable business range. | Native database controls reject terminal revisions before they become corrupt accepted evidence. Other technical counters retain their own contracts. |
| Lifecycle, identity and eligibility | Retain core-owned advisory eligibility, expected-revision checks, exact operation-byte hashing and receipt-first replay. Disposition/privacy authority remains separate from business facts. Domain decisions do not prove acceptance or bank transfers. | Existing Application, history replay, recovery, schema and actor-bound tests remain required. No adapter rule or new recovery identity. |

## Alternatives and implementation

A public smart-scalar API for every input would move authored spelling and error reporting onto clients and complicate exact retry identity. A generic command engine would obscure the nine explicit operations and introduce speculative extensibility. Merely patching two date checks would leave repeated parsing, contradictory metadata and stale durable rule evidence. Choose explicit internal validated inputs and shared accepted-state validation, retaining the simple public assertion contract.

Implement the reviewed internal model, date policy and semantic/durable revision together; update callers, native tests, generated inventories, contract lock, baseline catalog and documentation. Qualify corrections with an independent group/state matrix, clock rollback with changed/unchanged date controls, and revision/rule evidence against real isolated PostgreSQL. Run required verification and publish the exact tested revisions for review.
